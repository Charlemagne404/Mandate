# World continuity development cycle

Recorded 2026-10-02. This cycle expands the gameplay-depth alpha into persistent crises, multilateral consent, economic pressure, measurable strategy, government tenure and a player-facing strategic workspace. It preserves deterministic typed commands and atomic SQLite turns. It does **not** establish that real governments are intelligently simulated: the available local 3.2B model showed major planning failures, and the demo still favors funded projects over sophisticated autonomous diplomacy.

## World intelligence and continuity

Important planners receive a structured decision dossier: interests and benefits, resource cost, military/domestic exposure, trust and recent conduct, commitments, policy/red lines, relevant crises, economic dependencies, sanctions, conferences, alternatives and uncertainty. NationPlan records concise material decision factors and bounded confidence: confident, uncertain, internally divided or information lacking. Hidden chain-of-thought is neither requested nor stored. Demo divided governments delay conference consent; red lines can produce narrower terms.

Every active goal declares an evaluation method. Existing metric signals remain deterministic; additional criteria support relationships, membership, control/ownership, project completion, composite dependence and explicit capacity proxies. The global genesis has measurable criteria for all 242 goals. Legacy unmeasured goals migrate to visible capacity proxies, which are a compatibility fallback rather than proof that broad influence goals were substantively attained. Failed/cancelled projects fail dependent goals. Priority, elapsed stalled time and bounded pressure feed planning. Resource/domestic constraints can defer lower-priority goals. Terminal parent goals reconcile subgoals and mark related strategy for review. Government changes mark ongoing goals for review.

Automatic adoption of fresh long-term objectives after success, richer qualitative/composite proxy authoring and government deliberation remain shallow. Review markers are observable requests for reconsideration; they do not themselves invent a new strategy. Goal-dependent demo actions now continue beyond generic comfortable thresholds, avoiding domestic stability goals that stalled merely because unrest was already low.

Negotiated obligations still require explicit structured terms and independent consent. Breaches/fulfillment remain canonical and influence relations, retrieval and crisis pressure. Low-value routine consultations can be ignored when they have no concrete benefit, obligations or relevant goal. Same-turn reciprocal duplicate offers are vetoed. Recency penalties discourage meaningless repeated action families, while preferred goal priority/pressure can justify sustained investment.

Autonomy combines global rotating fairness slots with regional, authored major-power and crisis attention. Global genesis names a bounded strategic actor set; the engine does not hardcode real countries. Regional neighborhoods, meaningful relations, goal targets and exposed trading partners inform attention. Major powers no longer depend only on a changing synthetic capacity rank. Large worlds still spread low-salience actor attention across a long cycle. Quiet internal turns need not invent externally visible drama.

## Diplomacy and peace

Conferences support 3–20 explicit parties for security, trade, mediation, peace and sanctions. Parties independently accept, reject, counter, abstain, delay or withdraw. Counteroffers increment the round and invalidate earlier consent. Rejection/withdrawal close the proposal; dates expire unresolved proposals. Histories and final-state consistency are invariant checked. The UI distinguishes status, terms, parties, responses and current round.

Unanimous security/mediation/trade consent forms general organizations; security membership is a political coalition, not an automatically enforced military guarantee. Trade conferences create missing directional links and subsequently improve alternative access with modest fiscal effects. Sanctions conferences apply the coalition's validated sanctions. No real-world organization is required.

Peace conferences require every active belligerent plus optional mediators/stakeholders. Supported mechanical concessions are withdrawals and ownership transfers, revalidated against current control/ownership at final consent. Ending a war closes incompatible pending peace offers through existing settlement mechanics. The player UI currently offers status quo peace conferences; richer mechanical concessions are available through typed commands. Aid/reconstruction, binding guarantees, demilitarization, recognition, access and ceasefire monitoring are not completed conference mechanics. Exceptions and abstention do not count as consent to a partial coalition.

## Persistent crises

Canonical crises include finite type/status, participants and interested actors, regions, start/deadline, visibility, trigger/issues, demands/red lines, military posture, rhetoric, diplomatic breakdown, linked war/negotiations and bounded history. Severity is an inspectable weighted 0–100 index. Mobilization costs resources; warning, talks, stand-down, concession and freezing have bounded consequences. One actor cannot spam several crisis actions within a turn.

Crises persist, evolve with elapsed time and commitments, and expose severity/context to planners. Demands can require acknowledgment, ended war, ceasefire, actual control or accepted negotiation. Mechanical demands resolve from canonical facts; an actor cannot satisfy them by claiming success. All demands plus sufficiently reduced posture/breakdown are required for resolution. The seeded Orinoco crisis can therefore end when its related costly conflict ends. An unresolved Nordic demand can remain unresolved even after military pressure falls; de-escalation is not silently equivalent to agreement.

Overview contains a crisis workspace with severity meter, dimensions, participants, demands and condition labels, red lines, timeline and explicit actions. Major crises enter relevant briefings/history. There is no arbitrary drama generator or scripted escalation outcome. Autonomous creation of new crises is permitted by bounded capabilities but was not demonstrated in the completed global demo run.

## Economic networks and war

Directional links represent imports, exports, energy, strategic goods, finance, alternatives and adaptation as abstract indices. Global starting links and election data are fictional authored simulation inputs, not empirical economic/political datasets. Sanctions cover trade, finance, energy, strategic and military goods, with exposure, coalition breadth, alternatives, duration and resilience affecting target costs. Issuers also pay opportunity costs. War disrupts links. Adaptation develops over simulation months and substitution projects accelerate it. The sanctions UI exposes sector/intensity/purpose and relief controls. Coercion tests show dependence-sensitive damage, issuer cost, adaptation and relief; The bounded real coercion pilot selected energy substitution, then additional projects; it did not negotiate relief or seek alternative trade.

Wars can contain multiple theaters with permanent region references, posture, allocation, logistics, supply pressure, initiative and progress. Finite actions include hold, reinforce, limited/major offensive, withdrawal, air/naval pressure and ceasefire preparation. Force allocation is bounded; a theater actor cannot also spend the same abstract force through the legacy front command in one turn. Ceasefire blocks offensive theater action.

Code resolves capacity, readiness, logistics, industrial support, exhaustion and seeded variation; models cannot declare victory. Offensive progress is bounded, control changes only when a theater reaches its threshold, and at most one region changes per resolved capture. Even the legacy overwhelming offensive requires multiple actions. Legal ownership remains distinct from military control. Existing war costs compound resource, project, exhaustion, domestic and economic constraints. Theater air/naval effects remain coarse; terrain, blockade, external aid networks and operational surprise need deeper mechanics.

## Politics and information

Optional government tenure schedules elections by simulation date, with incumbent/challenger broad options and issues. An explicit deterministic approval/legitimacy/stability/economic/unrest aggregate decides retention versus a challenger; turnover changes the authored strategy, preserves promises/directives and marks goals for review. This is a lightweight continuity mechanic, not a partisan election model. Scenario data deliberately avoids fabricated real-world leader biographies. Rich leader traits, succession, serious-condition coups/revolutions and mortality events are not implemented.

Knowledge records represent confirmed or uncertain disclosure/ally sharing for private negotiations, treaties, conferences, crises, organizations and events. Only a knowledgeable issuer can share. Confirmed rooted disclosure grants access; likely/suspected reports are visible uncertainty and do not unlock the secret body or permit onward disclosure. Private organizations/conferences/crises are filtered from foreign planner/advisor/player views. Owner/developer evidence and the raw local debug API remain privileged canonical inspection surfaces; this is gameplay knowledge scoping, not encryption against the save owner. Leak/discovery incident generation and secret economic channels are not implemented.

## Timelines and player experience

Saved sibling/direct-parent branches can be compared semantically: ownership separately from control, wars, treaties, crises, governments, goals, commitments, relations, dependencies, sanctions, conferences, projects, public capacities and major events. Player-scoped private data is filtered. Comparison checks common ancestry and uses selected saved snapshots, not raw SQL rows. Arbitrary date replay, deeper ancestry traversal and map side-by-side/A-B highlighting are absent.

Overview adds compact canonical briefing categories: domestic, diplomacy, security, economy, commitments, opportunities and risks. Eight structured advisor queries cover commitments, threat indicators, stalled goals, known refusal history, economic dependents, escalation, project overload and recent major events. These report player-known facts; threat indicators are not claims to read a foreign cabinet's hidden intentions. No rich conversational advisor or AI prose synthesis is claimed.

Observer mode releases the player country to autonomous planning, supports repeated advancement/pause through existing autoplay controls and lets the user take control of an actor without restarting. The inspection perspective is retained; observer mode is not automatic omniscience. Important timeline events are filtered by major significance and player/selected country, goal targets and related regions; complete known history stays queryable. Directives now have visible critical/high/medium/low priority. They inform context and do not secretly execute player actions. The country workspace now has Overview, Diplomacy, Strategy, Economy, Military, Domestic, Projects and History sections. It separates goals/directives from promises, displays directional dependencies and tenure/conflict/history facts, and respects known-event scope. Bilateral details expose dependencies, known promises/treaties, grievances, trends and interactions. Structured standing orders, a graphical relationship trend workspace and notification inbox remain absent.

Scenario rules configure turn days, AI activity, crisis sensitivity, economic severity, seed and enabled new mechanics without code forks. Monthly accounting and date-driven elections/commitment/negotiation/deadline handling preserve elapsed time. Variable turn sizes are exercised by time-partition tests; the main interface still favors conventional strategic increments. Validated version-3 scenario files can be authored/exported, but there is no GUI scenario editor.

## AI efficiency and reliability

Quality presets bound total calls (fast 24, balanced 48, deep 80), with larger deliberate background attention rather than universal per-country calls. Traces record role, latency, prompt/context size, retries/repair and available provider token usage. Relevant mutable subsets are rebuilt for accuracy. Immutable output contract schemas are cached and frozen per orchestrator; tests verify schema reuse and fresh mutable red lines. Full provider prefix/scenario caching and incremental context diffs are not implemented.

Format/schema version 3 and migrations 005/006 persist the new entities, visibility and observer state. Validated legacy v1/v2 imports migrate without discarding history. New mutations use typed WorldCommands, sequential domain checks, invariants, provenance and atomic commits; core imports no provider, Electron, SQLite or Node functionality. Narration occurs after commit. Existing installed campaign genesis is preserved rather than silently overwritten by updated packaged scenarios.

Fault injection covers before requests, planning/proposals, validation, transaction entry/entity writes/commit boundaries, narration and memory update. Seven SQLite exception stages verify exact rollback or complete postcommit state; four actual child-process SIGKILL stages verify restart. Source and packaged desktop smoke SIGKILL during a deliberately stalled fake-compatible HTTP request and reopen with the exact original hash. These are actual process termination tests, not real intelligence calls. Every packaged phase, power loss, disk-full and corrupt-disk recovery are not certified.

## IMPLEMENTED AND TESTED

Typed/persisted crises and canonical resolution conditions; independent multilateral rounds/coalitions and bounded peace terms; directional dependencies and sector sanctions/adaptation; explicit goal evaluation/pressure/conflict/reconciliation; bounded theaters/seeded resolution/pacing; scheduled elections/strategy change; rooted knowledge sharing; observer control transfer; semantic saved-branch comparison; canonical briefings/structured advisor; visible directive priority and relevance filtering; real-provider discovery/benchmark harness; call budgeting/schema cache; transactional fault injection and actual process death.

## IMPLEMENTED BUT SHALLOW

Demo personality and long-term strategic replacement; capacity-proxy influence goals; election aggregate and named government continuity; economic indices and alternative access; coarse air/naval combat; political coalition enforcement; uncertainty effects outside constrained examples; second-order actor relevance; private observer perspective; nested branch detail rendering. Global demo activity remains heavily project driven and does not establish an entertaining 30–50-turn strategic narrative. Terminal objectives can leave countries without new ambitious aims.

## DESIGNED BUT NOT IMPLEMENTED

Full structured peace vocabulary with guarantees/reconstruction/access/monitoring, partial coalition exceptions, rich leadership traits and succession, validated standing orders, provider prefix/context-diff cache, map branch comparison, deeper historical ancestry traversal, broad qualitative goal evaluation, graphical relationship trends and a scenario editor. Available typed inputs or UI placeholders are not presented as completed mechanics.

## NOT ATTEMPTED

Model installation/download, paid inference without configured credentials, tactical units, detailed party simulation, serious-condition coup/revolution system, espionage/discovery minigame, external economic datasets, signed/notarized release, Windows/Linux package execution and exhaustive power-loss/disk-corruption testing.

## Next direction from observations

The tested local 3.2B model is not reliable for full strategic gameplay under the measured budgets; concurrent full-pipeline requests timed out even when compact single-choice requests worked. Bound provider concurrency and action-space/output size by demonstrated model capability, then compare a capable already-available provider against the same acceptance spectrum, full benchmark and reproducible pilots; keep model/configuration differences explicit. The more pressing gameplay weakness observed in deterministic worlds is that countries finish or stall narrow objectives and then return to recurring investments. Build a bounded strategy-reassessment cycle that selects replacement objectives from changed interests/resources, seeks specific partners, generates crisis/conference proposals only from canonical pressures, and measures 30–50-turn actor narratives. Test the resulting histories with real inference and human review. Broader event variety without rooted goals would obscure this weakness rather than resolve it.
